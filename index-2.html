元宵节

Lantern Festival

Lunar calendar 15th day, 1st lunar month · usually Feb or early Mar

Origin

The Lantern Festival falls on the first full moon of the new year and closes the Spring Festival celebrations. "Yuan" means the first month and "xiao" means night. Lighting lanterns is usually traced back to the Han dynasty, and by the Tang and Song dynasties city lantern fairs had become huge public events.

Customs

  • Viewing lanterns
  • Lantern riddles
  • Eating sweet rice balls
  • Dragon and lion dances

Tangyuan or yuanxiao?

Both are sweet glutinous rice balls. Southern tangyuan are wrapped by hand around a filling, while northern yuanxiao are made by rolling the filling in dry rice flour until it grows a coat.

The east wind blows open a thousand trees of blossoms in the night, then shakes them down like falling stars. Xin Qiji, on the Lantern Festival night · Song dynasty

Festival 2 of 4

端午节

Dragon Boat Festival

Lunar calendar 5th day, 5th lunar month · usually June

Origin

The best-known story honours Qu Yuan, a poet and minister of the state of Chu in the Warring States period. When Chu fell, he drowned himself in the Miluo River. Local people rowed out to search for him and threw rice into the water so the fish would leave his body alone. That is said to be the origin of dragon boat racing and zongzi.

Customs

  • Dragon boat racing
  • Eating zongzi
  • Hanging mugwort and calamus
  • Sachets and five-colour threads

Sweet or savoury?

Northern China tends to eat sweet zongzi with red dates or bean paste, while the south prefers savoury ones with pork or salted egg yolk. In 2009 the Dragon Boat Festival was added to UNESCO's list of Intangible Cultural Heritage.

Who first named this day Duanwu? For ages the story has said: it is for Qu Yuan. Wen Xiu, "Duanwu" · Tang dynasty

Festival 3 of 4

七夕节

Qixi Festival

Lunar calendar 7th day, 7th lunar month · usually August

Origin

Qixi comes from the legend of the Cowherd and the Weaver Girl. A weaver from heaven married a mortal cowherd, and the Queen Mother of the West separated them with the Milky Way. Moved by their love, magpies gather once a year on the seventh night of the seventh month and form a bridge so the couple can meet.

Customs

  • Praying for skill in needlework
  • Stargazing
  • Threading needles by moonlight
  • Eating "skill" pastries

They are real stars

The Weaver Girl is the star Vega in Lyra and the Cowherd is Altair in Aquila. On summer nights they shine on opposite sides of the Milky Way. Today Qixi is often called Chinese Valentine's Day.

One meeting of golden wind and jade dew outshines countless meetings in the human world. Qin Guan, "Immortals at the Magpie Bridge" · Song dynasty

Festival 4 of 4

中秋节

Mid-Autumn Festival

Lunar calendar 15th day, 8th lunar month · usually Sept or early Oct

Origin

The 15th of the eighth month sits right in the middle of autumn, which gives the festival its name. The most famous legend is Chang'e, who swallowed an elixir and floated up to the moon, where she lives with the Jade Rabbit. Wu Gang is also up there, forever chopping an osmanthus tree that heals itself.

Customs

  • Moon viewing
  • Eating mooncakes
  • Family reunion
  • Lanterns and osmanthus wine

Why mooncakes?

A round mooncake stands for a complete, reunited family. People far from home miss their families most on this night, which is why it is also called the Reunion Festival.

I only wish we may all live long, and share this lovely moon though a thousand miles apart. Su Shi, "Prelude to Water Melody" · Song dynasty
